1. WHAT THE EXISTING PAPERS DID (AND DID NOT DO)
================================================================================

Before building our own experiment, we studied five related papers. Here is
what each one did, explained clearly, along with what each paper did NOT do.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 FaceXBench (Johns Hopkins University)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What they built:
  The first comprehensive benchmark for testing multimodal LLMs on face
  understanding. They created 5,000 multiple-choice questions covering 14
  face-related tasks: age estimation, gender prediction, race estimation,
  high- and low-resolution face recognition, celebrity identification,
  anti-spoofing, deepfake detection, attribute prediction, expression
  recognition, head pose estimation, face parsing, crowd counting, and
  tool retrieval.

How many models tested: 28 (including GPT-4o, GeminiPro 1.5, and many
  open-source models of different sizes).

Best result: Qwen2-VL-72b at 57.86%. Humans scored 70.28%. Specialized
  vision models scored 84.50%. Even the best LLM is far behind humans.

Key finding for us:
  Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting made performance WORSE, not better.
  This means we should not assume CoT will automatically help with
  fingerprint tasks either. We test it anyway to confirm.

What they did NOT do: Anything with fingerprints.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2 FaceRecBench (Idiap Research Institute, Switzerland)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What they built:
  A benchmark specifically for face verification (same person or not?) using
  the exact same evaluation protocol as the traditional face recognition
  community — making scores directly comparable to classical FR numbers, unlike
  FaceXBench's MCQ format.

Best result: Qwen2-VL-7B at 81.10% across 27 models on 6 datasets. The
  specialized IResNet-50 scored 97.31% — a 16-point gap remains.

Key findings for us:
  - Many LLMs collapsed to 50% accuracy (always saying yes or always no).
    We must check for the same collapse in our SLAP tasks.
  - The best-performing LLM showed the worst racial bias. We need to watch
    for demographic bias across skin types and participant backgrounds.

What they did NOT do: No fingerprint analysis. No fine-tuning. Zero-shot only.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3 FPBench (New York University) — Most Relevant Prior Work
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What they built:
  The first-ever benchmark for testing multimodal LLMs on single-finger
  fingerprint analysis. Eight tasks: pattern classification, minutiae
  analysis, orientation flow analysis, fingerprint verification, sensor
  classification, real vs. synthetic detection, ACE-V forensic analysis,
  and tools retrieval. Approximately 5,000 questions across 7 real and
  synthetic datasets.

Best result: Gemini 2.5 Pro at 57.01%. After fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-32b
  with LoRA (10-18 minutes on one GPU), accuracy jumped by up to 39%
  on some tasks.

Key findings for us:
  - Fine-tuning works incredibly well for fingerprint tasks with very
    little data and very short training time.
  - CoT prompting hurt most models on fingerprint tasks — consistent with
    FaceXBench.
  - Tools retrieval (text-only reasoning) scored 70-97%, showing LLMs
    understand fingerprint workflows even when they struggle visually.

THIS IS OUR MAIN GAP:
  FPBench only uses single isolated finger images. It does NOT include:
  - SLAP four-finger images
  - Finger segmentation within a multi-finger image
  - Finger position identification (index vs. middle vs. ring vs. little)
  - Rotation angle estimation
  - Hand laterality (left vs. right)
  These are the exact tasks unique to SLAP analysis. This is what we fill.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.4 A Fingerprint for Large Language Models (Shanghai University)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What this paper is actually about:
  Despite the title, this paper has nothing to do with biometric fingerprints.
  It uses "fingerprint" as a metaphor for a unique digital signature of an LLM
  model — an IP protection paper, not a biometrics paper.

Relevance to our research: Almost none. Included in literature review only
  because of keyword overlap. Does not affect our methodology.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.5 Multimodal LLM-Enhanced Biometric Authentication Framework (Pace University)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What this paper proposes:
  A conceptual framework for combining LLMs with multiple biometric modalities
  plus differential privacy and federated learning. Reports impressive numbers
  but does not present original experiments. Published in a low-tier journal.

Relevance to our research: Limited. Useful background on privacy considerations,
  but provides no methodology we can build on directly.

2. THE RESEARCH GAP: WHAT NOBODY HAS DONE YET
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 What HAS been done
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Face understanding with LLMs benchmarked across 14 tasks (FaceXBench).
  - Face verification with LLMs benchmarked using standard FR protocols
    (FaceRecBench).
  - Single-finger fingerprint analysis with LLMs benchmarked across 8 tasks
    (FPBench).
  - Fine-tuning open-source LLMs on fingerprint data shown to work well
    (FPBench).
  - Conceptual frameworks for LLM + biometric authentication proposed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 What has NOT been done — our opportunity
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Nobody has tested LLMs on SLAP (four-finger) fingerprint images.
  - Nobody has asked an LLM to segment individual fingers from a four-finger
    slap image.
  - Nobody has asked an LLM to identify which finger is which
    (index, middle, ring, little).
  - Nobody has asked an LLM to determine left hand vs. right hand from a
    slap image.
  - Nobody has tested LLM performance on fingerprint rotation angle estimation.
  - Nobody has compared LLM slap segmentation against NIST SlapSeg baselines.
  - Nobody has fine-tuned LLMs specifically on SLAP segmentation and
    identification tasks.
  - Nobody has evaluated SLAP quality assessment by LLMs across NFIQ levels.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 Benchmark Comparison Table
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Capability                          FaceXBench  FaceRecBench  FPBench  SLAPBench
  --------------------------------------------- ------------ -------- ---------
  SLAP four-finger images                  No          No         No       YES
  Finger segmentation (multi-finger)       No          No         No       YES
  Finger position ID (index/middle/…)      No          No         No       YES
  Hand laterality (left vs. right)         No          No         No       YES
  Rotation and alignment estimation        No          No         No       YES
  NIST SlapSeg protocol alignment          No          No         No       YES
  Quality-stratified analysis (NFIQ)       No          No         No       YES
  Segmentation challenge identification    No          No         No       YES
  Single-finger fingerprint analysis       No          No        Yes    Optional
  Fingerprint verification (genuine/imp.)  No          No         No       YES
  Face understanding tasks                Yes         Yes         No        No
  Fine-tuning evaluation               Limited        No        Yes       YES

In summary: every existing paper benchmarks LLMs on either faces or isolated
single-finger prints. The four-finger SLAP format — the most operationally
significant fingerprint capture mode in border security — has never been
evaluated with any multimodal LLM. That is the gap we fill.


================================================================================
3. RESEARCH QUESTIONS
================================================================================

  RQ1: Can multimodal LLMs correctly identify and localize individual fingers
       within a SLAP four-finger image?

  RQ2: Can multimodal LLMs correctly label each finger (index, middle, ring,
       little) and determine whether the image is from a left or right hand?

  RQ3: How do different multimodal LLMs compare in assessing the quality of
       SLAP fingerprint images?

  RQ4: Can multimodal LLMs estimate rotation angles and alignment quality of
       individual fingers within a SLAP capture?

  RQ5: Does domain-specific fine-tuning improve LLM performance on SLAP tasks,
       and by how much?

  RQ6: How do LLM-based SLAP analysis results compare against traditional
       algorithmic baselines (NIST SlapSeg participants)?

  RQ7: Can multimodal LLMs perform fingerprint verification on SLAP images —
       correctly distinguishing genuine pairs (same person, multiple impressions)
       from impostor pairs (different people)?

4. DATASET
================================================================================

  Primary source:   NIST Special Database 302b (SD302b)
                    876 SLAP four-finger images with ground-truth bounding boxes
                    per finger. Already downloaded and organized in dataset/.

  Fallback:         Synthetic SLAP composites from FVC single-finger datasets
                    (only if more images are needed beyond what SD302b provides).

  Target size:      4,000 to 5,400 MCQ questions from the 876 SLAP images
                    (~5-6 questions per image on average across 7 tasks).

  Ground truth      Hand label (left/right) — from FRGP code in filename
  already in        Per-finger bounding boxes — from segmentation CSVs
  dataset/:         Rotation angles (theta) — from segmentation CSVs
                    Finger position (FRGP 2/3/4/5) — from segmentation CSVs

  Ground truth      NFIQ 2 quality scores — must run NFIQ 2 on all images
  to be             Segmentation challenge flags — heuristic from bounding box
  generated:          geometry + manual inspection + errata cross-reference

  Balance:          Equal left/right hands, balanced quality levels,
                    balanced A/B/C/D answer option distribution.

  Data split:       70% test | 20% fine-tuning train | 10% fine-tuning validation

  Known issues:     Cross-reference all images against dataset/ERRATA_SD302.txt
                    before generating questions. Exclude or flag participants
                    with known mislabeled fingers (e.g., 00002534 device S has
                    swapped thumbs; 00002420 device R has swapped slap segments).

  FRGP codes used in SLAP images:
    13 = Plain right four fingers (index, middle, ring, little) — PRIMARY
    14 = Plain left four fingers (index, middle, ring, little) — PRIMARY
    15 = Plain two thumbs — SECONDARY

  Segmentation CSV columns (ground truth per finger per image):
    filename, frgp, tlx, tly, trx, try, blx, bly, brx, bry, theta
    where tl/tr/bl/br = top-left/top-right/bottom-left/bottom-right corners,
    theta = rotation angle in degrees.

5. EVALUATION TASKS
================================================================================

All tasks use multiple-choice format (MCQ), following FaceXBench and FPBench,
for standardized and reproducible evaluation.

STATUS KEY:
  [ACTIVE]   — current priority, being worked on now
  [PLANNED]  — fully designed, will be implemented after active tasks complete
  [NEEDS REDESIGN] — task design issue identified, requires adjustment before use

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Task 1: Finger Count Detection  (target: 400-600 questions)  [NEEDS REDESIGN]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What we test:
  Can the model correctly count how many fingers are visible in a SLAP image?

Why it matters:
  In real captures, a finger can be lifted off the scanner, or a thumb can
  accidentally appear. The model must recognize these deviations.

Ground truth source: FRGP code (13 or 14 → 4 fingers; 15 → 2 thumbs).

Example question:
  "How many fingers are visible in this SLAP fingerprint image?"
  (A) 2   (B) 3   (C) 4   (D) 5

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Task 2: Hand Laterality Classification  (target: 400-600 questions)  [PLANNED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What we test:
  Can the model determine whether the SLAP image is from a left or right hand?

Why it matters:
  In border processing, knowing which hand was scanned is essential for
  correctly matching fingers to their database position
  (left index vs. right index are different enrolled identities).

Ground truth source: FRGP code (13 → right hand; 14 → left hand).

Example question:
  "This SLAP fingerprint image shows fingers from which hand?"
  (A) Left hand   (B) Right hand

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Task 3: Finger Position Identification  (target: 800-1,000 questions)  [PLANNED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What we test:
  Given a SLAP image where one finger is highlighted, can the model correctly
  identify which finger it is?

Why it matters:
  This is the core of slap segmentation. After locating each finger, the
  system must know which is index, middle, ring, and little.

Ground truth source: frgp column in segmentation CSV
  (2 = right index, 3 = right middle, 4 = right ring, 5 = right little;
   7 = left index, 8 = left middle, 9 = left ring, 10 = left little).

Example question:
  "The highlighted finger in this SLAP image is:"
  (A) Index finger   (B) Middle finger   (C) Ring finger   (D) Little finger

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Task 4: Finger Segmentation Localization  (target: 600-800 questions)  [PLANNED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What we test:
  Can the model identify the approximate spatial region of a specific finger
  in the SLAP image?

Why it matters:
  Tests spatial awareness. Can the model understand the left-to-right
  arrangement of fingers? (In a right-hand slap: little, ring, middle, index
  from left to right.)

Ground truth source: Bounding box coordinates in segmentation CSV.
  Relative x-position of the bounding box determines spatial order.

Example question:
  "In this right-hand SLAP image, where is the index finger located?"
  (A) Leftmost position   (B) Second from left
  (C) Third from left     (D) Rightmost position

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Task 5: Image Quality Assessment  (target: 600-800 questions)  [PLANNED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What we test:
  Can the model assess overall quality of a SLAP capture and identify
  specific quality problems?

Why it matters:
  Quality assessment determines whether a capture needs to be redone. In
  border settings, operators need quick, interpretable feedback.

Ground truth source: Must generate NFIQ 2 quality scores by running the
  NFIQ 2 tool on all slap-segmented images. Quality bands: good / medium / poor.
  Visual quality issues (blur, smudging, faint ridges) require labeling.

Example question:
  "What is the primary quality issue in this SLAP image?"
  (A) Motion blur   (B) Excessive pressure / smudging
  (C) Dry / faint ridges   (D) No quality issue

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Task 6: Rotation and Alignment Assessment  (target: 400-600 questions)  [NEEDS REDESIGN]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What we test:
  Can the model estimate whether and how much fingers are rotated relative
  to the vertical axis?

Why it matters:
  Rotated fingers degrade segmentation accuracy and matching performance.
  Operators need to detect and report misaligned captures.

Ground truth source: theta column in segmentation CSV (rotation in degrees).
  Binned into ranges: 0-10°, 10-25°, 25-45°, >45°.

Example question:
  "What is the approximate rotation of the fingers in this SLAP image?"
  (A) 0-10 degrees   (B) 10-25 degrees
  (C) 25-45 degrees   (D) More than 45 degrees

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Task 7: Segmentation Challenge Identification  (target: 400-600 questions)  [PLANNED]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What we test:
  Given a SLAP image with challenges (overlapping fingers, missing finger,
  thumb included, partial capture), can the model identify what makes
  segmentation difficult?

Why it matters:
  Real-world SLAP captures are messy. Operators need to know why segmentation
  failed so they can recapture correctly.

Ground truth source: Heuristic from bounding box geometry (overlapping
  bounding boxes → overlapping fingers) + errata cross-reference + manual
  inspection for edge cases.

Example question:
  "What segmentation challenge is present in this SLAP image?"
  (A) Two fingers are overlapping   (B) One finger is missing
  (C) A thumb is accidentally included   (D) No challenge detected

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Task 8: Fingerprint Verification — Genuine vs. Impostor  (target: 600-1,000 pairs)  [ACTIVE]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What we test:
  Given two SLAP images, can the model determine whether they are from the
  same person (genuine pair) or two different people (impostor pair)?

Why it matters:
  This is the most operationally important task in biometrics. It directly
  answers: "Is this the same person?" SD302b is uniquely suited because:
    - Multiple impressions of the same finger exist (Device R vs. Device S,
      500 PPI vs. 1000 PPI, and potentially multiple collection days).
    - 201 participants provide a large impostor pool.

Background — Impression and Pair Types:
  An "impression" is a single captured SLAP image. The same person's hand
  will produce slightly different images each time it is pressed.

  Genuine pair:   Two impressions from the SAME person.
                  Example: Subject 00002406 Device R vs. Subject 00002406 Device S.
                  Expected: model says "same person." VeriFinger score > 48.

  Impostor pair:  Impressions from TWO DIFFERENT people.
                  Example: Subject 00002406 Device R vs. Subject 00002430 Device R.
                  Expected: model says "different people." VeriFinger score < 48.

  VeriFinger threshold: Score > 48 = match (same person); < 48 = not a match.
  This threshold is used as the algorithmic ground truth baseline to compare
  against LLM responses.

Ground truth source:
  Primary: Same subject_id = genuine pair; different subject_ids = impostor pair.
  Algorithmic baseline: VeriFinger SDK match scores (threshold = 48).

Pair construction from SD302b:
  Genuine pairs: Same participant, Device R (500 PPI) vs. Device S (500 PPI),
                 or R_500 vs. R_1000 (same device, different resolution),
                 or Day 1 vs. Day 2 impression (if multi-day captures available).
  Impostor pairs: Random sample of different-participant pairs, same FRGP code.
  Balance: Equal number of genuine and impostor pairs to avoid bias.

Example question:
  "Below are two SLAP fingerprint images. Are these from the same person?"
  (A) Yes, same person   (B) No, different people

Evaluation metrics:
  Overall accuracy, False Accept Rate (FAR), False Reject Rate (FRR),
  Equal Error Rate (EER) — standard biometric verification metrics.
  Compare directly against VeriFinger algorithmic baseline.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Total target: 4,600 to 6,400 questions/pairs across all eight tasks.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Task-to-Ground-Truth Summary:
  Task 1  Finger Count         FRGP code in filename
  Task 2  Hand Laterality      FRGP code in filename (13=right, 14=left)
  Task 3  Finger Position      frgp column in segmentation CSV
  Task 4  Localization         bounding box (tlx/trx) in segmentation CSV

  Task 6  Rotation             theta column in segmentation CSV

7. PROMPTING STRATEGY
================================================================================

How prompts are phrased matters enormously. Both FaceXBench and FPBench found
that different strategies produced significantly different results. We use
three settings to understand the full picture.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Setting 1 — Zero-Shot (Primary Evaluation)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  System prompt:  "You are an expert fingerprint examiner."
  User prompt:    "Please answer the question and provide only the correct
                  option letter, e.g., A, B, C, D."

  This is our primary evaluation setting. No context is added beyond the
  image and question.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Setting 2 — Task Description
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Same as zero-shot, but we prepend a brief task-specific description.

  Example for Task 3 (Finger Position ID):
    "A SLAP fingerprint image captures four fingers from one hand pressed
    simultaneously on a scanner. In a right-hand slap, from left to right,
    the order is: little, ring, middle, index."

  Purpose: Tests whether grounding the model in domain knowledge improves
  spatial reasoning on SLAP-specific layouts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Setting 3 — Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  User prompt:  "Please think step by step and provide your reasoning before
                giving the final answer. Include the final correct answer
                option at the end of your answer."

  Based on FaceXBench and FPBench, CoT likely hurts performance on visual
  fingerprint tasks. We test it to confirm whether this holds for SLAP tasks.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Consistency Rules
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Every prompt documented word-for-word.
  - Temperature = 0 for deterministic output.
  - Each model run 3 times; mean and standard deviation reported.
  - Response parsing uses VLMEvalKit's three-step option extraction:
      Step 1: Match option letter at start of response.
      Step 2: Search for option letter anywhere in response.
      Step 3: Compare response text against option values directly.


================================================================================
8. EVALUATION METRICS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 Primary Metrics
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Overall Accuracy:   Percentage of questions answered correctly across all tasks.
  Per-Task Accuracy:  Accuracy broken down by each of the seven tasks.
  Per-Model Accuracy: Performance comparison across all tested LLMs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.2 Detailed Metrics
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Precision, Recall, F1-Score:
    Computed per answer option, especially for Task 3 (finger position ID —
    how often does the model confuse index for middle, etc.).

  Confusion Matrix:
    For finger identification tasks, shows which fingers get confused with
    which. Expected confusions: middle↔ring (adjacent fingers, similar shape).

  Accuracy by Quality Level:
    Performance stratified by NFIQ 2 bands: good / medium / poor.
    Tells us whether models degrade on low-quality captures like humans do.

  Accuracy by Rotation Range:
    Performance as finger rotation (theta) increases.
    Tells us whether models struggle more with rotated fingers.

  Model Collapse Detection:
    Explicitly check if any model always picks the same answer option
    (e.g., always answering "Right hand" for Task 2). Flag all collapsed models.

  Answer Distribution Analysis:
    Check whether models show bias toward specific options (A/B/C/D frequency).
    Target distribution: ~25% each for four-option, ~50% each for binary.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.3 Comparison Baselines
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Random chance baseline:   25% for four-option MCQs, 50% for binary questions.
  Frequent choice baseline: Accuracy from always picking the most common answer.
  NIST SlapSeg baselines:   Compare LLM results against published SlapSeg
                            algorithm performance where available.

9. FINE-TUNING
================================================================================

FPBench showed that fine-tuning open-source models on fingerprint data produced
7-39% improvements per task with only ~10-18 minutes of LoRA training on a
single GPU. We replicate this methodology for SLAP images.

  Method:           LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) on both vision projector and
                    LLM backbone — same setup as FPBench.

  Models:           2-3 best-performing open-source models from zero-shot
                    evaluation results.

  Training data:    ~800-1,000 MCQ examples from the 20% training split.

  Training time:    Expected 10-20 minutes per model on a single H100/H200 GPU
                    (based on FPBench with similar dataset size).

  Evaluation:       Fine-tuned models evaluated on the same 70% test set,
                    compared directly against zero-shot results.

  Expected gains by task (based on FPBench patterns):
    Task 3 (Finger Position ID):    Highest gain — spatial layout is learnable.
    Task 2 (Hand Laterality):       High gain — left/right have opposite orderings.
    Task 4 (Localization):          Moderate gain — spatial reasoning improves.
    Task 5 (Quality Assessment):    Moderate gain.
    Task 6 (Rotation Estimation):   Limited gain — needs diverse rotation examples.

10. STEP-BY-STEP TIMELINE (12-16 Weeks)
================================================================================

  Phase 1 — Dataset Acquisition and Exploration  (Weeks 1-2)
    - SD302b is already downloaded. Inspect file structure, image resolutions,
      naming conventions.
    - Identify all 876 usable SLAP images (FRGP 13 and 14) from devices R and S.
    - Cross-reference all images against ERRATA_SD302.txt. Flag and exclude
      participants with known mislabeled or swapped fingers.
    - Verify segmentation CSV ground truth for all SLAP images.

  Phase 2 — Data Preparation and Question Generation  (Weeks 3-4)
    - Run NFIQ 2 quality scoring on all slap-segmented images (Task 5 ground truth).
    - Generate challenge flags from bounding box geometry (Task 7 ground truth).
    - Design question templates for all seven tasks.
    - Generate the full MCQ question set: target 4,000-5,400 questions.
    - Balance A/B/C/D answer distribution across all tasks.
    - Split data: 70% test / 20% fine-tuning train / 10% fine-tuning validation.
    - Ensure no participant appears in both train and test splits.

  Phase 3 — Evaluation Framework Setup  (Weeks 5-6)
    - Set up VLMEvalKit on compute environment.
    - Download and configure all open-source models (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3,
      Gemma3, LLaVA-OneVision).
    - Set up API access for GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro.
    - Write and finalize all prompt templates for all tasks and settings.
    - Run pilot test on 50-100 questions to verify parsing and outputs.

  Phase 4 — Zero-Shot Evaluation (Main Experiment)  (Weeks 7-9)
    - Run all models on all questions across all three prompting settings.
    - Temperature = 0. Each model run 3 times. Log all raw outputs.
    - Compute all metrics: accuracy, precision/recall/F1, confusion matrices.
    - Check every model for collapse behavior and option bias.
    - Document any model that refused to answer or returned invalid output.

  Phase 5 — Fine-Tuning Experiments  (Weeks 10-11)
    - Select 2-3 best-performing open-source models from zero-shot results.
    - Fine-tune each using LoRA on the training split.
    - Evaluate fine-tuned models on the test split.
    - Compare fine-tuned vs. zero-shot on all 7 tasks.

  Phase 6 — Analysis and Paper Writing  (Weeks 12-14)
    - Compile all results into tables and figures (mirror FPBench style).
    - Perform quality-stratified analysis (good vs. medium vs. poor images).
    - Perform rotation-stratified analysis (low vs. high theta values).
    - Analyze failure cases: which images do models get wrong, and why?
    - Write the paper following the structure of FaceXBench and FPBench.

  Phase 7 — Review and Submission  (Weeks 15-16)
    - Internal review and revision.
    - Prepare supplementary materials (question templates, prompts, sample images).
    - Release code and benchmark on GitHub.
    - Submit to target venue: IEEE T-BIOM (primary), CVPR, or ECCV.

11. WHAT MAKES THIS A NOVEL CONTRIBUTION
================================================================================

  First in the field:
    No prior work has evaluated any multimodal LLM on SLAP four-finger
    fingerprint images.

  Entirely new task categories:
    Finger segmentation, finger position identification, hand laterality
    classification, and rotation estimation from multi-finger images have
    never been benchmarked with LLMs.

  Operationally relevant:
    SLAP processing is used at every US border entry point. These are real,
    consequential tasks used in deployed border security systems.

  Methodologically grounded:
    We use the same tools (VLMEvalKit), question format (MCQ), and prompting
    strategies as FaceXBench and FPBench. Our results are directly comparable
    to prior work — showing exactly where SLAP tasks are harder or easier than
    face and single-finger tasks.

  Extends FPBench into multi-finger territory:
    FPBench showed that fine-tuning dramatically helps for single isolated
    finger tasks. We test whether this holds for the harder, more complex
    multi-finger SLAP domain.

  Dataset already in hand:
    Unlike many benchmarks that require new data collection, SD302b is already
    downloaded. Ground truth for 5 of 7 tasks is directly readable from the
    existing segmentation CSV files. This significantly de-risks the project.

  First LLM fingerprint verification benchmark on SLAP images:
    No prior work has asked any multimodal LLM to distinguish genuine SLAP pairs
    (same person, multiple impressions) from impostor pairs (different people).
    We provide FAR/FRR/EER analysis and compare directly against VeriFinger
    algorithmic baseline scores, creating a concrete bridge between LLM evaluation
    and classical biometric verification protocols.
